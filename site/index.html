What it does

Every account.
Then the next step.

Most usage meters stop at the number. Burn keeps going: whether it lasts, whether today is unusual for you, which account has room, and a command that puts you on it.

group

As many accounts as you have

Several Claude accounts side by side — work, personal, a client's — plus the rest of your plans, read from the sign-ins already on your Mac. Sign in right in the app for anything missing; nothing to paste.

Claude ChatGPT Grok Gemini Cursor Copilot
insights

A forecast per window New

Session, daily, weekly, monthly — and model caps like Fable — each with where it lands by the reset, and one verdict per account.

local_fire_department

Unusual, for you

A banner when a window burns at 3× your own typical busy hour, or a model does 3× a typical day's work — 2×, 3× or 5×, your call. Measured against your week, nothing to set up.

system_update_alt

Updates itself New

Burn looks for a new version once a day and installs it only if it carries Burn's own signature. It never throws a window over your work.

terminal

Launchers

A command per Claude account — claude-atlas, claude-studio — that runs Claude Code as that account. Hover a row for Open Terminal with this account.

data_object

A CLI and a status line

burn prints what the panel knows, burn json feeds scripts, and burn statusline puts the right account in Claude Code's status line.

cloud_off

Their outage, not your sign-in

Anthropic, OpenAI, Cursor and GitHub status pages are checked every five minutes. An incident is one line at the top of the panel, a click from the status page.

history

90 days of history, your way

Every account's charts in Settings → Usage, from a day to three months. Pin more than one ring to the menu bar, rename accounts, hide the ones you don't need, and set quiet hours for the notifications — a banner at your threshold, and another when a low window resets.

Install

Two ways in.
Both take a minute.

Download

Signed with a Developer ID and notarized by Apple. Open the disk image, drag Burn to Applications, press ⌥Space. From then on it keeps itself up to date.

downloadDownload Burn.dmg

Homebrew

The cask lives in a tap. It's marked as self-updating, so the app handles new versions and brew upgrade leaves it be.

$brew install --cask tmass1/tap/burn
Runs on what you haveReads the sign-ins Claude Code, Codex, the Grok CLI, Antigravity, Cursor and the GitHub CLI keep on your Mac. Nothing goes anywhere but each vendor's own usage endpoint.
No account, no telemetryBurn has no server. Its data lives in ~/Library/Application Support/Burn and nowhere else.
